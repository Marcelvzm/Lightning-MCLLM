# Drum & Bass / Liquid — Concept Proposal

*BPM 165–180. Fast but feels SMOOTH. Audience moves at half-time
(~85 BPM perceived). Continuous flow, beautiful drops.*

## 1. Identity

DnB lives in a paradox: the kick-snare pattern at 170 BPM gives
**16th-note motion** with a **half-time feel** because of syncopation.
The audience moves at half the BPM. The lights work in this same dual
register: detail at 1/16 rate, structure at 1/2 rate.

Subgenres:

| Subgenre | Character |
| --- | --- |
| **Liquid** | Smooth, jazzy, melodic, often beautiful pads |
| **Neurofunk** | Technical, bass-heavy, clinical |
| **Jump-up** | More "fun", melodic stabs, often cartoonish bass |
| **Rollers** | Groove-driven, hypnotic, minimal |
| **Hardcore-DnB hybrid** | More aggressive, closer to hardcore territory |

This concept covers **liquid / rollers** as the default. Neurofunk
trends colder; jump-up trends warmer and more energetic; adapt as
needed.

## 2. Audience perceptual state

* Continuous engagement (not waiting for a drop)
* Faster body movements but felt as smooth, not aggressive
* Audience is often FOCUSED — many DnB fans are technical listeners,
  paying attention to bass design, drum programming
* Tolerant of long sections, beautiful drops, and detailed visual work
* Crowd is often dancing in pairs/groups rather than mass-jumping

## 3. Macro structure

A typical 6-minute liquid DnB track:

```
0:00 ── 0:30 ── 1:30 ──── 3:00 ── 3:30 ──── 5:00 ──── 6:00
intro    drop1   sustain   break   drop2     sustain    outro
                                   (main)
ENT: low → high → high → low → highest → very high → low
```

**Drops in DnB are ENTRIES, not eruptions.** The bass enters and the
track unlocks; nothing "explodes". This is structurally important for
lighting design.

Sets are 60–120 minutes. Energy modulation is gradual. Across a 90-min
set, the floor rises 20%, plateaus, peaks twice, declines.

## 4. Palette philosophy

DnB likes COOL SATURATED colours with occasional warm contrasts.

| Sub-aesthetic | Palette |
| --- | --- |
| **Default liquid** | Deep cyan, magenta, deep blue. Smooth crossfades. |
| **Neurofunk** | Cold mono — white, deep blue, lime UV. Almost techno-like. |
| **Jump-up** | Brighter — magenta, orange-cyan contrasts. More playful. |
| **Liquid pastel** | Sunset colours — pink, gold, cyan. RARE in dance music; DnB liquid permits these. |

**Highlight**: white, used as smooth additions, not snaps.
**Warm contrast**: amber for occasional moments — used MUCH more
sparingly than in techno.

Colour rotation is faster than techno but smoother than hardtekk —
**colours CROSSFADE rather than snap**. A 2–4 second crossfade is the
genre's signature transition.

## 5. Motion vocabulary

* **Continuous wave motion** — a phase-shifted chase running constantly
  across the rig. **The defining motif of the genre.**
* **Snare hits** — every 2 beats, a small punctuation (often a tiny
  sweep or a flash on accent layer)
* **Bass drops** — brief but bigger reactions
* **Atmospheric drift** during breaks

The continuous wave is the genre's signature. Light should always be
GENTLY MOVING in DnB — even in calm moments. **Stillness reads as
broken** in this genre.

## 6. Time grid

DnB has TWO grids the lights can pick:

1. **Half-time grid** (BPM/2) — feels natural, matches audience movement
2. **Full grid** (BPM) — for fast accents, only briefly

Most lighting events sit on the half-time grid (every 2 beats = roughly
0.7 seconds at 170 BPM). Fast accents on the snare (every 2 beats from
a different phase) add detail.

The 16th-note hat layer is **too fast for lighting to follow** — don't
try. Lights flickering at hi-hat rate looks anxious, not energetic.

## 7. Hierarchy of fixture roles

| Layer | % of rig | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Atmosphere wave** | 30–40% | Runs the continuous wave. The genre's foundation. |
| **Snare punctuation** | 15–25% | Hits on the 2 and 4 of each bar. Small, white-ish flashes. |
| **Bass response** | 15–25% | Activates on bass drops, sustained colour or rolling motion |
| **Beam architecture** | 10–20% | Always on, slowly moving, defines space |
| **Accents** | 5–10% | Rare peak moments |

The CONTINUOUS quality of the rig is what distinguishes DnB. Multiple
layers running simultaneously, each at different frequencies, creates
the perceived smoothness.

## 8. Section archetypes

### State D1: "The Wave"
Atmosphere wave running continuously. Beam architecture lit. Snare
punctuation OFF. Bass response OFF. The "calm-but-moving" state.

### State D2: "Drop Sustain"
D1 + bass response active. Snare punctuation activates. Wave intensifies
slightly. **Most of the track lives here.**

### State D3: "Break"
D1 stripped further. Often single beam, deep colour, very slow drift.
Atmospheric pads in the music = atmospheric in light too. 16–32 bars
typically.

### State D4: "Drop Hit"
First 1–2 bars of a drop. Brief brightness peak, atmosphere shifts
colour, beam architecture might add a sweep. **NOT a hardstyle drop —
more a beautiful arrival.**

### State D5: "Peak Section"
The biggest part of the track. All layers active. Wave at full
intensity, snare punctuation crisp, bass response saturated. Maybe an
accent layer briefly fires.

A track typically traces:

```
D1 → D4 → D2 → D5 → D3 (break) → D4 → D5 (main drop) → D1 (outro)
```

## 9. Drop / peak architecture

DnB drops are ENTRIES, not detonations. Design:

1. **Build** = 8–16 bars, often with a riser/filter sweep + light
   intensifying. The riser/filter audio sweep is BEAUTIFULLY paired with
   a parallel light brightness sweep.
2. **Drop hit** = bass enters, light marks with a brief peak (1–2 bars)
   — colour shift, brightness lift, snare punctuation activates
3. **Sustain** = 16–32 bars in D2/D5 territory
4. **Outro** = pulling back to D1 over 4–8 bars

The second drop in a track is bigger than the first — but the
difference is often **subtle**: maybe 10–20% more intensity, a new
colour introduced, an additional sweep. NOT dramatically bigger like
hardstyle.

## 10. Reset and breathing

Breaks in DnB are GORGEOUS. They are part of the show, not a pause.

A break should:

* Lower atmosphere significantly (40–60% reduction)
* Stop snare punctuation
* Either still wave or stop wave entirely (depending on track's
  break design)
* Often introduce a NEW colour — a moment of "we are here now" before
  the drop
* Last the duration of the musical break (typically 8–32 bars)

A great DnB break is a moment of **suspended motion** — the wave is
gone, a single beam holds a deep colour, the audience leans in.

## 11. Recurring motifs

* **The wave** — never stops in active sections. It IS the genre.
* **The snare punctuation** — every 2 beats, a tiny flash. Audience
  anchors to this.
* **The bass-drop colour** — a specific colour used ONLY on bass-drops.
  Audience learns the visual signal.
* **The break beam** — single beam in fog during breaks. Recurring
  "calm" image, mirrors techno's break beam.
* **The pre-drop riser swell** — light brightness rises in parallel with
  the audio's filter sweep over 8 bars.

## 12. Anti-patterns

* **Static lighting** — DnB demands continuous motion. Stillness reads
  as broken.
* **Trying to hit every snare** — snare every 2 beats AT 170 BPM is too
  fast for big effects. Use small hits or accept that not every snare
  gets a flash.
* **Hardstyle-style drops** — DnB drops are smoother. Full-rig white
  hit with strobe at the drop is wrong here.
* **Wrong palette** — pure red/orange palettes are unusual for DnB.
  Cooler tones are the genre.
* **Sync-locked perfection** — the genre swings; lighting should too.
  Quantized-perfect sync feels rigid.
* **Strobing during breaks** — breaks are CALM. Strobe is for peaks.

## 13. Signs it's working

* Audience moves continuously (not start-stop like hardstyle)
* Drops feel like ARRIVALS, not eruptions
* Breaks feel like part of the show, not pauses
* Photos taken anywhere in the track look good — there's no "dead time"
* The DJ leaves the track playing through to the end (a sign the energy
  is sustained)
* The crowd vibes — body language flowing, not posturing

# Hardstyle / Hardcore / Rawstyle — Concept Proposal

*BPM 150–200. Drop-driven, peak spectacle. Lighting matches the spectacle.
Restraint is intentional, deployed at specific moments to make the
spectacle BIGGER.*

## 1. Identity

Three closely related genres:

| Genre | BPM | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hardstyle** | 150–160 | Melodic, anthemic, drops are CATHARTIC |
| **Rawstyle** | 150–160 | Less melodic, distorted, drops are AGGRESSIVE |
| **Hardcore** | 160–200 | Maximum intensity, less melodic, rhythmic punishment |

All three share: **PEAK-DRIVEN DESIGN**. The drop is the show. Tracks have
near-formulaic structure (intro → build → drop → break → build → drop →
outro) and a CULTURE that EXPECTS spectacle.

The lighting must MATCH the spectacle. Restraint is not the answer here.
But intentional restraint at the right moment makes the spectacle bigger.

## 2. Audience perceptual state

* Maximum activation — heart rate, sweat, group energy
* **Audience EXPECTS spectacle** — they are visually receptive
* Group behaviour: synchronised jumping, fist-pumping, vocalising
* Attention is high but easily fatigued by sustained chaos
* **Wants to KNOW when the drop is coming** — reads the buildup
* Tolerance for ordinary states is LOW — the audience came for peaks

This last point is crucial. The audience READS the buildup and EXPECTS
the payoff. The lights MUST signal both.

## 3. Macro structure

Tracks are 4–6 minutes, drop-driven:

```
0:00 ── 0:30 ── 1:30 ──── 2:30 ── 3:00 ── 3:30 ──── 4:30 ── 6:00
intro   build1  drop1     break   build2  drop2     outro
                          (rest)          (main)
ENT: low → rising → HIGH → low ──→ rising ──→ HIGHEST → fade
```

Sets are 30–90 minutes, building drop-to-drop. The drops themselves form
a meta-rhythm — typically 1–3 drops per track, 8–25 drops per set.

## 4. Palette philosophy

Hardstyle/hardcore wants SATURATED, AGGRESSIVE colour. White is for peak
hits.

| Role | Colours |
| --- | --- |
| **Primary brand** | Deep red — often the genre's signature |
| **Peak hit** | Pure white (held for drops only) |
| **Build colours** | Orange, magenta, deep blue |
| **Atmosphere** | Dark blue, near-black with fog, cool greys |
| **Break colours** | Often complementary to the drop palette — if drops are red, breaks are blue |

**Rawstyle / Hardcore variations**: more brutal — military green, harsh
white, deep red, blood-orange, lime UV. Less melodic, less "pretty".

**Forbidden**: pastels, washed-out tones, "Instagram" colours, anything
soft.

## 5. Motion vocabulary

* **Peak motion** = full-rig white-hits + fast beam sweeps + lasers (if
  available) + strobing
* **Build motion** = rising intensity over time, often a single chase
  whose rate increases (rate doubling every 4 bars is canonical)
* **Break motion** = SLOW or NONE. Resets the audience.
* **Vocal-only sections** = often near-blackout with single beam on the
  singer or visual focal point — theatrical

The genre PERMITS — even DEMANDS — visual chaos at drops. But it MUST be
**coherent chaos**: structured visual peaks, not random.

## 6. Time grid

Tightly locked. Drops happen on the bar line, not between. The "kick on
the one" of the drop is **sacred** — the most important sync moment in
the entire show.

Builds use:

* **Rate doubling** — every 4 bars, the strobe rate doubles
* **Filter sweeps mirrored in lighting** — slow rise in brightness over
  16 bars matching the audio filter
* **Anticipation cues** — ALL lights go to ONE colour and freeze for
  2 bars before the drop. Audience reads this with maximum certainty.

## 7. Hierarchy of fixture roles

| Layer | % of rig | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Atmosphere** | 20–30% | Defines the room. Simpler than techno (less drift). |
| **Pulse** | 25–35% | Beat-locked. |
| **Beam** | 20–30% | At peaks, big architectural moves. |
| **Accent / Weapons** | 5–15% | Strobes, blinders, peak-only fixtures. **Much larger than other genres.** This layer MAKES the drops. |

The accent layer's prominence is what distinguishes hardstyle rigs from
techno or hardtekk rigs. Reserve real fixtures for it.

## 8. Section archetypes

### State Z1: "Anticipation Halt"
Just before a drop. ALL lights go to a SINGLE colour (often deep red),
held for 2–4 bars. Maybe a single beam moves slowly. The audience READS
this as "the drop is in 2 bars". **Crucial** for build-drop emotional
payoff. Without it, drops feel less inevitable.

### State Z2: "The Drop"
The first 1–4 bars of the drop. Full-rig WHITE hit on the first kick.
Strobing on accent layer. Beams sweeping. THIS is the moment the show
pays off.

### State Z3: "Sustain"
Bars 4–16 of the drop. Slightly less intensity than Z2, but still
high. Pulse + beam + accent all active. Colour may shift from white to
red or magenta.

### State Z4: "Outro of Drop"
Final 4–8 bars of a drop section, gradually pulling back. Strobing
stops. Sweeps slow. Atmosphere darkens.

### State Z5: "Break"
Often beautiful. Atmosphere only. Single deep colour. One beam pointing
somewhere meaningful. Audience catches breath.

### State Z6: "Build Phase 1" (early)
Atmosphere rising slowly. Single chase added. No strobing yet.
Atmospheric — the audience knows energy is coming but it's still
pleasurable.

### State Z7: "Build Phase 2" (late)
Atmosphere bright. Pulse layer active at high intensity. Strobing
starts on accent layer at slow rate (1/4 beat or slower). Tension
visibly mounting.

### State Z8: "Build Phase 3" (final)
Strobing rate doubled. Beam layer adds sweeps. Atmosphere goes
WHITE-ish or dramatically saturated. Last 2 bars of build.

A typical drop architecture:

```
Z6 → Z7 → Z8 → Z1 (halt) → Z2 (DROP) → Z3 (sustain) → Z4 → Z5 (break)
```

## 9. Drop / peak architecture

The drop is the entire reason for the show. Design it BACKWARDS from
peak:

1. Decide what the BIGGEST moment will be (full rig + lasers + strobe +
   white)
2. Decide the build to it (16–32 bars of rising intensity, segmented Z6
   → Z7 → Z8)
3. Decide the anticipation halt (2–4 bars frozen on one colour)
4. Decide the first kick of the drop — RIGOROUSLY synced to the music's
   first kick. **This is the most-noticed moment.** Off-by-one beat is
   a disaster.
5. Decide the sustain (16–32 bars)
6. Decide the outro (4–8 bars pulling back)
7. Decide the break (16–32 bars of contrast)

Each drop in a track should be DIFFERENT in some way:

* **First drop**: introduce the visual peak vocabulary
* **Second drop**: BIGGER than the first — more strobe, more colours,
  lasers if available
* **(If a 3rd drop)**: again different — maybe slower, with more
  atmospheric beam work, or the BIGGEST one

The audience tracks "did this drop top the last one?". An LLM-authored
hardstyle show that uses identical drops feels FLAT.

## 10. Reset and breathing

Hardstyle MUST have breaks. Without them, the drops mean nothing. A
break should:

* Be 8–32 bars long
* Be substantially DARKER than drops (50–70% less intensity)
* Often be COLOURED differently (cold blue when drops were red)
* Be slower — less motion, longer fades
* Sometimes be silent visually (one beam, one colour, frozen)

A great hardstyle break is itself a beautiful image — not a "pause".

## 11. Recurring motifs

* **The white drop hit** — full-rig white on the drop's first kick. Used
  every drop, slightly different each time (colour variations, sweep
  additions). The signature event.
* **The build strobe escalation** — strobe rate doubles every 4–8 bars
  during builds. Audience subconsciously knows "doubling = drop coming".
* **The break beam** — during breaks, a single beam pointed up-centre,
  visible in fog. Recurring "altar" image.
* **The anticipation freeze** — that 2–4 bar pause before each drop. The
  audience LOVES this — it's the tension peak.
* **The post-drop fade** — every drop ends with a 4–8 bar fade-down. The
  rhythm of drop-fade-break-build-drop becomes the show's pulse.

## 12. Anti-patterns

* **Drop without buildup** — the audience hasn't earned it. Won't land.
* **Buildup without drop** — the audience feels cheated.
* **Continuous strobe** — kills the meaning of strobe at peaks.
* **Wrong colour for break** — if the drop is red and the break is also
  red, the contrast is weak. Use opposing colour.
* **Hesitant drop** — if the lighting "ramps into" the drop instead of
  slamming, the moment is lost. **Drops are SNAPS.**
* **Identical drops** — the second drop must differ from the first. The
  audience tracks this.
* **No anticipation halt** — without the freeze before the drop, the
  drop is less inevitable, the moment less owned.

## 13. Signs it's working

* Audience SCREAMS or jumps at drops
* The dancefloor feels physically pushed by the lighting
* Photos taken AT the drop look better than photos taken between
* The DJ goes off / makes hand-up gestures during your peaks
* After the set, audience members say "the drop on track X was insane"
* Builds are visibly tense — people stop talking to focus on the rig
* The break beam gets photographed (it always does)

## 14. Suggested motif inventory for an LLM

For each track, an LLM should generate at minimum:

* `<track>_atmos_drop` — atmosphere base for drop sections
* `<track>_atmos_break` — atmosphere base for breaks (different colour)
* `<track>_build_phase_1`, `_2`, `_3` — three escalating build chases
* `<track>_anticipation_halt` — the 2-bar freeze
* `<track>_drop_hit` — the full-rig white snap
* `<track>_drop_sustain` — the held drop chase
* `<track>_drop_outro` — the fade-down
* `<track>_break_beam` — the single-beam break image
* `<track>_lasers_peak` (if rig has lasers) — peak laser pattern

Then a bank with the build → drop → break sequence laid out across slots
1–7, with slot 9 = blackout.

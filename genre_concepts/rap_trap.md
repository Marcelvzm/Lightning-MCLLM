# Rap / Trap — Concept Proposal

*BPM 70–95 (often half-time feel). Vocal-led genre. The performer is the
show. Light supports them — never competes. Restraint is highest of any
genre, structure is about FOCUS rather than energy modulation.*

## 1. Identity

Rap and trap are **vocal-led genres**. The audience is listening to
WORDS. Lighting that demands visual attention COMPETES with the words.
Lighting that supports them AMPLIFIES them.

Subgenres:

| Subgenre | BPM | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Boom-bap / classic rap** | 85–95 | Sample-based, groove-driven, often warm |
| **Modern trap** | 65–75 (felt as 130–150 with 1/16 hats) | Half-time feel, sub-bass-heavy |
| **Drill** | 130–145 (felt as 65–75) | Aggressive, sliding 808s, dark atmosphere |
| **Conscious / lyrical** | varies | Atmospheric, often slower, theatrical lighting fits |
| **Hype / club rap** | 70–95 | Crowd-engagement-driven, brighter |

The defining feature across all: the performer is the focal point.
Lighting follows the performer's lead.

## 2. Audience perceptual state

* Part listening, part dancing, part watching the artist
* More cognitive engagement than dance genres (lyrics)
* Often filming with phones — **lights need to FILM well** — bright peaks
  at hooks, dark verses
* Expects HYPE moments at hooks, drops, ad-libs
* Less continuous group movement; more "hype moments" punctuating
  attentive listening
* Audience age range often wider than dance genres — affects production
  expectations

## 3. Macro structure

A typical rap song is 2–4 minutes:

```
0:00 ── 0:15 ── 0:45 ── 1:00 ──── 1:30 ── 1:45 ── 2:15 ── 2:30 ── 3:00
intro   verse1  hook    verse2     hook    bridge  hook    outro
        (calm,  (peak)  (calm)     (peak)  (vary)  (final  (fade)
         words)                                     peak)
```

Lighting structure mirrors VERSE-HOOK contrast. **The hook is the drop
equivalent.**

A live rap set is 30–90 minutes. Energy modulation comes from
track-to-track changes more than within-track changes. Hype tracks vs.
ballads vs. interludes form the macro arc.

## 4. Palette philosophy

Rap loves SATURATED, DEEP colours. Less variety per song than dance
genres.

| Section | Palette |
| --- | --- |
| **Verses** | Deep red, deep blue, dark amber. Mood-setting. Often "warm and serious". |
| **Hooks** | Saturated reds, golds, magentas. The "celebration" colours. |
| **Hype tracks** (energetic) | Warm amber + white, or deep red + white |
| **Conscious / sad tracks** | Deep blue, monochrome blue + white, sometimes single deep red |
| **Drill** | Harsh red + white only. Dark, threatening. |

**White is used as PUNCTUATION**, not as a base colour. Reserve for:
drop hits, ad-lib emphasis, specific punchlines.

**Forbidden**:

* Rainbow lighting on rap shows (looks pop-concert)
* Bright primary blue on a verse (looks unintentional)
* Strong purple unless it's a stylistic choice the artist signals

## 5. Motion vocabulary

**Mostly STILL.**

* 90% of the show should have NO active motion — fixed scenes
* Slow drift is acceptable (a 30-second tilt move from one position to
  another is fine)
* Fast motion is reserved for **hype moments** — a sweep on a hook
  entry, a strobe on a punchline
* NO hectic chases. The artist is the dynamic element; the lighting is
  the frame.

The DJ for the rapper might want some chase action between songs —
that's fine — but during the song, lighting calms.

## 6. Time grid

Looser than dance. Rap is rarely on the 1/16 grid for lighting purposes.
Instead, lights move on:

* **Punchlines** — predicted by the artist's flow
* **Hooks** — the entrance of a hook is a major light cue
* **Ad-libs** — the rapper's "yeah!", "huh!", etc. — often pre-known
  and pre-cued
* **Bass-drops** — when the 808 hits
* **Refrains** — any repeated line meant to be noticed

**Operator timing matters more than auto-timing.** Pre-program the song;
manually fire cues. An LLM authoring rap shows must understand: the
*track structure* drives lighting, not the *beat grid*.

## 7. Hierarchy of fixture roles

| Layer | Role | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Performer Spotlight** | 1–3 fixtures | MOST IMPORTANT. White or warm white on the artist. ALWAYS on during verses. Brightness goes UP on hooks. |
| **Atmosphere wash** | 30–40% of rig | Defines the BACKDROP. Usually dim, deep colour. Slow changes (per song or per section). |
| **Hook layer** | 15–25% | Activates on hooks. Adds colour, motion, intensity. Goes dim during verses. |
| **Punctuation layer** | 5–15% | For punchlines, ad-libs, specific beats. Mostly OFF. |
| **Hype layer** | 5–10% | Strobes, blinders for moments. Used 1–3 times per song max. |

**Spotlight is non-negotiable in any rap show. The artist must be
visible.**

## 8. Section archetypes

### State R1: "Verse — The Frame"
Spotlight on artist, atmosphere wash deep colour (low intensity), hook
layer OFF, punctuation OFF. Held for the entire verse, with possibly
ONE small change at the verse mid-point (e.g., wash slightly brightens,
or a single beam appears).

### State R2: "Hook — The Lift"
Spotlight stays. Atmosphere brightens or shifts colour. Hook layer
activates with a colour motif (e.g., gold wash). Punctuation might fire
on the hook's first line. Lasts the duration of the hook.

### State R3: "Bridge"
Often a vocal-only or stripped section. Even dimmer. Maybe just the
spotlight + one accent beam. Theatrical.

### State R4: "Drop / Punchline"
A 0.5–1 second flash on the punchline. White-hit on hype layer. Returns
to State R1 immediately. The audience screams.

### State R5: "Outro / End"
Atmosphere slowly fades. Spotlight remains. Theatrical.

### State R6: "Bass-drop / 808 hit"
Saturated colour wash flash on atmosphere (often deep red or magenta),
maybe a hype-layer pulse. 1 bar duration. Returns to verse state.

A track typically traces:

```
R1 → R2 → R1 → R2 → R3 → R2 → R5
verse hook  verse hook  bridge final hook  outro
```

With R4 (punchlines) interspersed during verses, R6 (808 hits)
interspersed during heavier sections.

## 9. Drop / peak architecture

Rap "drops" are SMALLER than dance drops. They are PUNCTUATION, not
eruption.

**A punchline drop:**

1. Spotlight stays
2. Hype layer fires WHITE for ~0.2–0.5 seconds
3. Maybe atmosphere flashes a saturated colour simultaneously
4. Returns to verse state immediately

**A hook drop (entering the hook):**

1. Spotlight intensifies
2. Hook layer activates (colour shift on atmosphere + new fixtures lit)
3. Maybe a single sweep on the entry kick
4. Held for the hook's duration

**A bass-drop (the 808 hit, common in trap):**

1. Brief deep-red wash on the atmosphere
2. Hype layer flashes red, not white
3. Holds 1 bar before settling

## 10. Reset and breathing

Rap NATURALLY has breath: between verses, dynamics drop. Lighting
follows naturally. A "verse ending" is a small lighting calm before the
hook.

NEAR-BLACKOUTS work for theatrical moments — useful for storytelling
rap, ballads, or bridges. The artist alone in the dark with a single
spot can be VERY powerful. Save for moments the artist signals (often
they cue you with body language or pre-show direction).

## 11. Recurring motifs

* **The hook lift** — the atmosphere always shifts the SAME WAY when
  the hook enters. The audience learns this and anticipates.
* **The punchline white-flash** — every punchline gets a 0.2s white
  flash. The artist relies on this — they may LOOK at the rig
  expecting it.
* **The verse-end transition** — between verse and hook, a brief
  darkening then brightening. A "breath".
* **The bass-drop colour** — a specific saturated colour reserved for
  808 / bass hits. Audience learns to expect it.

## 12. Anti-patterns

* **Bright hectic chases during verses** — Audience can't focus on
  lyrics. **Cardinal sin.**
* **The artist in the dark** — never let the spotlight die during a
  verse.
* **Over-using white-hit** — a flash on every line is a flash on no line.
* **Wrong-colour spotlight** — coloured spotlights on rap artists rarely
  work. White or warm white only, with rare exceptions for theatrical
  moments.
* **Too much motion** — the artist is the motion; the lights should be
  still.
* **Ignoring the artist** — a lighting designer who doesn't watch the
  performer can't predict cues. The performer is the conductor.
* **Treating it like EDM** — putting hardstyle drops on a rap show
  looks ridiculous.

## 13. Signs it's working

* Audience captures clear, well-lit videos of the artist
* Photographers can shoot crisp portraits during your show
* The artist makes eye contact, nods, gestures up — they feel supported
* Audience reactions are LOUDER than during equivalent dance-genre
  moments (because they can hear the lyrics over the energy)
* Hook drops get clear visible audience response (singing along, raising
  phones, fists)
* The artist trusts you enough to sometimes go quiet — a subtle gesture
  cues a blackout — silence punctuated by darkness

## 14. Suggested motif inventory for an LLM

* `<song>_verse` — the State R1 frame (spot + dim wash)
* `<song>_hook` — the State R2 lift (spot + brighter wash + hook colour)
* `<song>_bridge` — even dimmer state (often just spot + 1 accent)
* `<song>_punchline_flash` — 0.2s white-hit, fire-and-forget
* `<song>_808_hit` — bass-drop colour flash, 1 bar
* `<song>_blackout_moment` — for theatrical pauses, manual trigger
* `<song>_outro_fade` — long fade-down chase

For a SET (not single song), also:

* `set_intro_dramatic` — pre-show atmosphere before artist enters
* `set_walkout` — bright energy moment as artist enters
* `set_outro_lights_up` — house-lights moment at end

# Techno — Concept Proposal

*BPM 125–138. Audience in long-form dance trance. Light is part of the
room, not a competing entertainment.*

## 1. Identity

Techno is the genre of **trance-inducing repetition**. A 4/4 kick that
does not stop for 7–12 minutes per track, with subtle modulations of
texture, harmony, and percussion building and receding around it. The
audience is in a meditative state — often dancing for hours.

The track is not a story, it is a *texture being explored*. Energy
comes from accumulation of detail, not from drops.

The light show is **part of the trance**. It must NOT compete for
attention. It must FUNCTION as part of the room — like the smoke, the
bass, the bodies, the architecture.

> Light as building material, not as event.

## 2. Audience perceptual state

* Often closed-eyed, deep in the music
* Dancing with eyes occasionally open
* In a long-attention state — hours, not minutes
* Tolerant of monotony if the texture rewards it
* **Allergic to interruption** — overt drops, vocal stings, hectic visuals
* Photographers find it hard to capture (a sign you're doing it right)

Lighting that "pleads for attention" fails. Lighting that "rewards
attention" succeeds. The audience should be able to ignore the light for
five minutes, then notice that something has subtly changed and feel
rewarded.

## 3. Macro structure

A typical techno set is 1–3 hours. Within a single 7-minute track:

```
0:00 ────────── 1:00 ──────── 3:00 ──── 4:00 ── 5:00 ── 6:00 ── 7:00
   foundation      layering    breakdown re-entry  peak    outro
   (kick + perc)   (more       (kick      (kick    texture wind-down
                    layers)     drops)    returns) (peak)
   ENT: low ─────────────────► mid ────► low ───► mid ──► high ► low
```

The lighting mirrors this not by being "on" or "off" but by:

| Section | Lighting state |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00–3:00 | Gradual entropy increase. ONE thing changes per minute. |
| 3:00–4:00 | Breakdown gets MINIMUM lighting. Single beam, deep colour, near-stillness. |
| 4:00–5:00 | Re-entry. The light "answers" the kick by returning to its prior state. The audience FEELS the return. |
| 5:00–6:00 | Peak texture. ~50–60% of what hardstyle would call "peak". Brief flashes acceptable. |
| 6:00–7:00 | Wind-down mirrors intro. |

Across a SET, the energy floor slowly rises across 30–60 minutes, then
plateaus, then has occasional peaks. A good set is a **slow-moving line
graph**, not a series of spikes.

## 4. Palette philosophy

Techno wants restraint. Colour is a precision tool, not paint.

**Pick ONE primary palette per set, not per track.**

| Palette | Aesthetic | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cold mono** — white + deep blue + UV | Clinical, infinite, machine-room | Industrial techno, peak-time |
| **Warm mono** — amber + deep red + tungsten white | Hypnotic, ceremonial | After-hours, ritual sets |
| **Magenta-cyan** — saturated but tightly held | Berlin / Tresor aesthetic | Newer, more melodic techno |
| **Pure black-and-white** — dimmer-only, no colour | Sculptural, fog-dependent | Maximum minimalism |

**Secondary accent**: 1 colour, used rarely (≤5% of the set), as a peak
signal. The rarity is what gives it meaning.

**Forbidden**: rainbow rotation, RYGB sequencing, anything that looks
like a kindergarten or a top-40 EDM festival.

## 5. Motion vocabulary

Slow, with phase shifts.

**Allowed motion types:**

* Slow tilt drifts (8–32 seconds per movement)
* Phase-shifted pulse waves across the rig (0.2–0.5 second offsets per
  fixture group)
* Geometric figure formations (line, arc, V) held for long periods, then
  morphed
* Beam architecture: parallel beams creating volume, rotating slowly as a
  unit
* Strobing: ONLY at peak moments, ONLY in short bursts (1–4 bars)

**Forbidden motion:**

* Hectic chases that step every beat
* Random-looking moves (techno is deterministic)
* Fast pan/tilt sweeps in non-peak moments
* Continuous gobo rotation (reads as tacky)
* Anything that suggests a "moment" that the music isn't selling

**Rate of change**: at any moment, the lighting state should be
describable in one sentence. AND that sentence should change roughly
every 16–32 bars.

## 6. Time grid

Techno is on the grid. 100% of effects align to:

* Kick beats (always)
* Bar lines (every 4 beats — the strong unit)
* Phrase lines (every 8, 16, 32 bars)
* Section transitions (every 64–128 bars)

Off-beat work is rare and intentional — typically only on the "and" of
beat 4 leading into a new bar (a "lift") or on a syncopated percussion
accent the audience can already hear.

The pulse chase (4OTF) at low intensity, looping forever, is a foundational
element. Above it, slower changes happen at multiples of 4 bars.

## 7. Hierarchy of fixture roles

Every techno rig — regardless of fixture count — should have FOUR layers:

| Layer | % of rig | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Atmosphere** | 40–60% | Wide washes, slow colour/intensity drift, defines the ROOM |
| **Pulse** | 20–30% | Beat-locked dimmer movement. Breathes with the kick. Low intensity. |
| **Beam** | 10–20% | The architectural element. Visible beams in fog, defining lines and volumes. Slow rotation. |
| **Accent** | 1–3 fixtures | The rare-event layer. Used only at peaks, transitions, or to mark a single specific musical event. |

If a rig only has 4–8 fixtures, **collapse the layers**: 2 atmosphere +
2 pulse + 2 beam + 1 accent. Don't sacrifice the layered model — it's
what gives techno its depth.

## 8. Section archetypes

A track or set passes through these states:

### State A: "The Room Exists"
Single deep colour wash (atmosphere only). 30–60% intensity. No motion.
Maybe one beam pointed straight up, also deep colour. The audience feels
the ROOM more than the lights. Held 16–64 bars.

### State B: "The Pulse"
A + pulse layer adds 4OTF dim breathing. Atmosphere unchanged. Beat now
visible in light.

### State C: "Architecture"
B + beam layer activates with slow geometric formation (e.g., parallel
diagonals). No colour change yet. The room "fills" without getting
louder.

### State D: "Tension Buildup"
C + atmosphere starts a slow brightness creep over 16+ bars. Pulse layer
slightly more intense. Beam layer might add a slow rotation. Colour may
start drifting toward the accent palette.

### State E: "Peak Texture"
Brief (8–16 bars). Highest intensity of the track. Strobing on accent
layer in 1-bar bursts. Atmosphere bright. Pulse layer at full intensity.
Beams in active rotation. **NO chaos** — still locked to the grid.

### State F: "Breakdown"
The opposite of peak — often the most beautiful state. Atmosphere fades
to deep, beam layer drops to 1–2 visible beams, pulse layer pauses. The
room holds its breath. 8–32 bars.

### State G: "Re-entry"
A pull back into State C or D as the kick returns. Often the most
emotionally satisfying moment of a track.

A track typically traces:

```
A → B → C → D → E → F → C → D → E → A
```

A set adds a Z-level traversal: across tracks, the energy floor slowly
rises, plateaus, peaks once or twice, declines.

## 9. Drop / peak architecture

Techno does not have hardstyle drops. It has **emergence moments** — a
returning kick after a breakdown, a new percussion layer locking in, a
reveal of harmony.

Light marks them by:

* TINY changes the audience didn't see coming. Atmosphere shifts by 20%,
  a beam appears that wasn't there, a colour saturates 10% more
* RESTRAINT. A drop in techno is RARELY answered with full-rig white.
  Save full-rig white for the ONE moment of the set where it earns its
  meaning — typically the climax of the headline hour, used once.

## 10. Reset and breathing

There aren't "rest moments" the way hardstyle has them — but there are
**stripping-back moments**:

* Removing the beam layer for a phrase
* Going to atmosphere-only for a breakdown
* A near-blackout for ONE bar at a clear musical mark (e.g., between two
  tracks, on the moment of a kick removal)

The audience interprets these strips as **punctuation, not pauses**.

## 11. Recurring motifs

Throughout a techno set, certain visual signatures should recur:

* **The breathing wash** — the atmosphere layer's slow drift; present
  90%+ of the time
* **The beam constellation** — a specific fixed geometric shape used
  repeatedly with variations
* **The bar-line confirm** — a tiny brightness peak on the first beat of
  every 8 or 16 bars (barely perceptible but anchors the audience's
  sense of structure)
* **The "answer"** — when the music does X (e.g., snare entrance), the
  light always responds with the same Y. Consistency builds trust.

## 12. Anti-patterns

* **Treating it like a pop concert.** No big colourful intros. No
  spinning gobos. No multi-colour fast cycles.
* **Strobing as filler.** Strobe is an EVENT in techno, not a texture.
  Two strobes per track maximum.
* **Chasing every drum hit.** A kick gets a pulse. A hat does NOT get
  a flash. Honour the kick; ignore the rest.
* **"Spectacle thinking".** The audience didn't come to see the lights.
  They came to be IN the room with the music.
* **Ignoring breakdowns.** The breakdown is the most beautiful state.
  Many LLMs skip it because "nothing's happening musically". That IS
  the point — match it visually.

## 13. Signs it's working

* The DJ smiles or makes hand gestures at lighting moments
* The audience doesn't applaud or react to lighting changes — they keep
  dancing
* After 10 minutes, you cannot recall what specifically happened — but
  the room feels "right"
* A photographer says "everything looks good but it's hard to capture"
* People in the crowd close their eyes more than open
* A second DJ taking over doesn't immediately change the lighting style
  — meaning your style is right for the genre, not for one DJ

## 14. Suggested motif inventory for an LLM

When authoring techno scenes/chases, generate this minimum motif set:

* `<set>_room` — the State A wash
* `<set>_pulse` — the State B pulse chase, beat-anchored
* `<set>_arch` — beam layer architectural pattern (often a slow chase)
* `<set>_buildup` — the slow brightness creep chase
* `<set>_peak` — the rare full-energy state
* `<set>_breakdown` — the minimal state
* `<set>_punct` — the bar-line confirm (a tiny accent flash, fired once
  every 8 or 16 bars; can be a chase that fires once and stops)
* `<set>_blackout_one_bar` — a slot you can fire manually at section
  boundaries
